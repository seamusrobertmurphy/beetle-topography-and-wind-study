 Yes, it's possible. You can keep the submitted design, annual maps with sixteen-day models for the wind question, and add the spatial statistics on top of it. What you can't do is revert exactly, because the submitted analysis had defects that would carry straight back in.

      Adding the spatial statistics to the submitted design:
      - Point patterns. Run the L function envelopes, Clark-Evans and the bandwidth on the eight annual maps, and on the sixteen-day maps for the wind models.
      - Bandwidth and dependence. The submitted annual models already had last year's attack in the same cell and within 90 m. These can be refitted with the neighbourhood term weighted at each year's bandwidth and compared on AIC with the fixed radii, as I did in the revision.
      - Moran's I. Compute it on the residuals of the annual models M0 to M3 and of the sixteen-day models.
      - Scale. Add the grain test and the spatial field refit to the annual final model.

      All of this sits beside the submitted models without changing their time intervals.

      What should not be reverted:
      1. The wind field. The submitted MicroMet field covered only 18,388 of 61,923 cells, and every wind result in the submitted paper, annual and sixteen-day, rests on it. It has to be rebuilt either way.
      2. The annual classifier. The submitted annual maps came from the 38-plot SVM, kappa 0.475, which you rejected on 16 September. Meanwhile the submitted text describes the method of Murphy et al. (2026), so the code and the prose disagreed.
      3. The wind interaction's sign. The submitted −0.049 for stems × wind was read as less attack in thin, windy stands. With standardised terms, a negative interaction means more attack there than the main effects alone predict. That needs checking before the finding is reused.

Two cautions:
- The submitted findings may not survive these fixes, whatever design we use. Adding dependence terms shrank every environmental effect in the revision, and it will do the same to the annual models.
- Choosing an analysis because its results read more strongly is a risk if a reviewer asks why the design was chosen. The safer defence is your own design statement: annual maps for the three questions and sixteen-day maps for wind, with the defects fixed and the new tests added. Whatever that produces is then the result.
